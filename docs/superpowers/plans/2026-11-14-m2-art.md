# M2 Plan A: art Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task, in
> one session, with no subagents (project rule). Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A character, a tile and a backdrop are drawn from pictures a game's own files describe: a `figure` file and
a `backdrop` file are painted into PNGs, the same bytes on every machine and in every browser, and the game plays
them (clips, auto-tiled ground, layers that drift, a camera that follows the players).

**Architecture:** Two new kinds of content file, `figure` and `backdrop`, join plan C's table. A small painter in the
`content` feature turns each into a picture and an atlas with nothing but whole-number and `dmath` arithmetic and the
repo's own PNG writer, so it runs in Node and in a page and gives the same bytes in both. Which pose a character
shows is the sim's: a `Sprite` component, switched on by the game's content, that the `render` feature only reads.
`render` gains pictures (sprites, a tile layer, backdrop layers) and a camera; a game with no figure, no backdrop and
no `view` has the sim and the picture it has today.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript 7.0.2 strict, Zod 4.6.6, PixiJS 8.22.0, Koota 0.6.6,
`@dimforge/rapier2d-deterministic-compat` 0.21.0, Vitest 5.0.3 (browser mode on Playwright 1.63.0), Biome 2.5.15,
knip 6.39.0, pnpm 10.34.6, Node 24.21.0. No new dependency: the PNG writer is ours.

**Spec:** [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-m2-design.md`](../specs/2026-11-14-m2-design.md), "Art (plan A)", the
content table's `backdrop`, `figure` and `character` rows, "M1's deferred minors" (M7 and M8) and the camera
sentence of "Multiplayer in the demo", on top of
[`2026-11-13-2d-first-layer-design.md`](../specs/2026-11-13-2d-first-layer-design.md). Principles served: 1 (art is a
JSON file an agent writes, checked in plain sentences and painted by one command), 2 (a figure is parts with a place
and a size, a backdrop is layers with a height: what a finger drags in the M3 builders), 3 (2D only), 5 (the
builders' painting half arrives here: a figure file and a scene file, painted free and the same every time), 6
(PixiJS draws; nothing is added to the stack), 7 (the old painter is read and its rules kept; see "Old code").

   build one kind of thing, a person, as a 3D body seen from eight sides and lit; it paints with `Math.sin` (the old

   keeper, a critter, crates, sparks, a door and the ground, in four colours, seen from the side. So a `figure` here
   is parts (blocks and rounds) with poses, and it paints anything: a character, a tile, a prop. What is carried
   over is the builders' idea and the painter's rules (see "Old code"). The options, as sent to the coordinator:
   (a) these formats; (b) port the old painter's body, gait and outfit data cut to a side view, with its
   dependency, as a larger plan; (c) these formats now, and (b) as a second painter behind the same `figure` kind in
   M3, when the figure builder is built.
2. **The painter is in the `content` feature and has no page and no Node in it,** so `automata art`, a test in
   WebKit and the M3 builders on an iPad all paint the same bytes. No new layer: the order stays sim, physics,
   commands, net, content, level, server, render, input, audio, saves, host, locale, story, ui, screens, web, game.
3. **A tile and a prop are figures too.** A tileset's `image` may be a figure's picture (each pose is one square),
   and a backdrop's prop is a figure standing on a layer. One painter, one format to learn.
4. **The pose is the sim's.** A cutscene (plan T) runs in the sim and names an actor's clip, so which clip plays has
   to be sim state. The `Sprite` component holds the clip and the pose it shows now; `render` draws that pose and
   decides nothing. It is in the hash, so a replay shows the same picture on the same tick.
5. **Switched on by content.** The sim's schema hash covers every component, so `Sprite` joins a sim only when a
   character has a `figure` or a level has a `view`. Tile boxes are joined only in a level with a `tileset`. The
   demo's level today has walls of stacked rows, so joining them for every level would move both of Examples'
   goldens.
6. **`push` is asked for, not guessed.** The clips `idle`, `run`, `jump`, `fall` and `land` follow from how a body
   moves. Whether a hero is pushing depends on what it touches, which the game's own code knows: it calls
   `setClip(hero, 'push')`.
7. **The renderer works the frames out from the figure file,** with the function the painter used, so the two
   cannot disagree and a game fetches one file per picture. The atlas beside each picture is for people and other
   tools, in the frames-and-meta shape PixiJS and TexturePacker read.
8. **The camera is switched on by a level's `view`.** Without it the whole level is in view, as today. With it the
   camera shows that much of the level around the heroes and pulls back, as far as the whole level, to keep them all
   in. It moves once per tick, never by the clock, so a picture taken at a tick is always the same picture.
9. **Player 2, 3 and 4 are told apart by a figure's `variants`:** the same figure in other paints.
10. **A picture is checked two ways.** `artProblems` compares bytes (for `automata check` and CI). At run time a
    missing picture, or one of the wrong size, stops the game with a sentence; the game never repaints quietly.

### Task 1: A PNG, written by us

No package and no `node:zlib`: zlib's bytes may change with a version of Node, and a page has none.

**Files:**
- Create: `src/lib/png.ts`
- Test: `tests/lib/png.test.ts`, `tests/lib/png.parity.test.ts`, `tests/features/render/png.browser.test.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces: `IndexedPicture`, `encodePng` (the contract). Throws a `RangeError` with a plain sentence for a size
  out of range, more than 256 colours, a `pixels` of the wrong length or a pixel past the palette's end.

The file, exactly: the 8-byte signature; `IHDR` (bit depth 8, colour type 3, no interlace); `PLTE` (three bytes per
entry; entry 0 is `0, 0, 0`); `tRNS` (one byte, `0`: entry 0 is clear and every other entry solid); one `IDAT`;
`IEND`. Each row is filter byte `0` and then its pixels. The zlib stream is `0x78 0x01`, one deflate block (final,
fixed Huffman codes), and the Adler-32 of the rows. The block is made greedily: at each byte, take the longest
match of 3 to 258 bytes at distance 1 (the byte before) or at distance `width + 1` (the same place one row up),
the longer of the two and distance 1 when they tie; a match is written when it is 3 or longer, else the byte as a
literal; then the end-of-block code. CRC-32 over each chunk's type and data.

**`figure`** (noun "figure"): a picture made of parts, and the poses it can take. It paints a character, a tile or
a prop. It is drawn facing right.

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `format`, `id` | |
| `tile` | whole, 4 to 64: how many of the picture's pixels make one tile in the game |
| `frame` | `{ w, h }`, whole, 4 to 128: one pose's size in pixels |
| `outline` | opt, colour: an edge one pixel wide round the whole figure |
| `paints` | 1 to 16 of `{ id, color }`: the colours the parts use, each named for what it is for |
| `parts` | 1 to 32, drawn first to last (the first is at the back), of `{ id, shape, x, y, w, h, paint, turn? }` |
| `parts.shape` | `block` or `round` |
| `parts.x`, `parts.y` | the part's middle, in pixels from the frame's left and top; 0 to 128 |
| `parts.w`, `parts.h` | more than 0, at most 128 |
| `parts.paint` | `Id`: one of `paints` |
| `parts.turn` | opt, −180 to 180 degrees, clockwise, about the part's middle |
| `poses` | 1 to 64 of `{ id, moves }`; a pose with `"moves": []` is the figure as its parts say |
| `poses.moves` | at most 32 of `{ part, dx?, dy?, turn? }`; `dx`, `dy` −128 to 128 pixels; `turn` −180 to 180, added to the part's own |
| `variants` | opt, at most 8 of `{ id, paints }`: the same figure in other colours. Player 2's hero is drawn in the first, player 3's in the second, player 4's in the third |
| `variants.paints` | 1 to 16 of `{ id, color }`: only the paints that change |

paint `id`); `There is no part called "hed". This figure has: body, head.`;
`"head" is moved twice in this pose. Put its moves together.`;

**Where frames go.** Frames are the poses in order, then each variant's poses in order, named `<pose>` and
`<variant>/<pose>`. Frame `n` is at column `n % 8`, row `floor(n / 8)`; the picture is `min(frames, 8) * frame.w`
wide and `ceil(frames / 8) * frame.h` tall.

**The palette.** `[clear, ...paints in order, outline if there is one, ...each variant's paints in order]`.

**How one frame is painted.** For each part, first to last: its middle is `(x + dx, y + dy)` and its turn
`turn + the move's turn`, in degrees. The pixel in column `c`, row `r` of the frame is the part's when the point
`(c + 0.5, r + 0.5)`, turned back by that angle about the part's middle, lies in the shape: for a `block`,
`|lx| <= w / 2` and `|ly| <= h / 2`; for a `round`, `(lx / (w / 2))² + (ly / (h / 2))² <= 1`. A turn of 0 uses no
`sin` or `cos` at all, so an unturned figure is exact whole-number work. A later part paints over an earlier one.
Then the outline: every pixel of the frame still clear, with a painted pixel directly above, below, left or right
of it inside the frame, takes the outline colour (worked out from the frame as it was before any outline pixel was
set). A variant's frame is painted the same way with its paints in place of the ones it names.

### Task 5: Paint a backdrop

**Files:**
- Create: `src/features/content/paint-backdrop.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `Picture` (Task 4), `createRng`, `deriveSeed`, `sin` from the `sim` feature.
- Produces: `paintBackdrop`, `backdropSheet` (the contract).

The picture is `width * tile` pixels wide. Each layer is one strip, `round(height * tile)` pixels tall (at least 1),
the first layer at the top of the picture. The palette is `[clear, each layer's colour in order]`. Props are not in
the picture: they are figures, drawn by the renderer.

Each strip is painted from its own stream, `createRng(deriveSeed(seed, layer.id))`, with the draws in the order
written here, where `W` and `H` are the strip's size in pixels and `fill(c, top)` paints column `c` from row `top`
(clamped to 0 and `H`) down to the bottom:

- `flat`: every pixel.
- `hills`: `a = 1 + rng.int(2)`, `b = 3 + rng.int(3)`, `p = rng.next() * 2π`, `q = rng.next() * 2π`; for each column,
  `t = 2π * (c + 0.5) / W` and `fill(c, round(H * (0.5 - 0.3 * sin(a * t + p) - 0.15 * sin(b * t + q))))`.
- `mountains`: `n = 3 + rng.int(3)` peaks; for each peak `k` in order, `x = (k + 0.2 + 0.6 * rng.next()) * W / n`,
  `tall = H * (0.55 + 0.4 * rng.next())`, `half = (W / n) * (0.6 + 0.3 * rng.next())`; a column's height is the
  greatest, over every peak and over the peak moved by `-W`, `0` and `W`, of `tall * (1 - |c + 0.5 - x| / half)`,
  and never less than `0.15 * H`; `fill(c, round(H - that))`.
- `clouds`: `n = 3 + rng.int(4)` clouds; for each, `x = rng.next() * W`, `y = H * (0.25 + 0.4 * rng.next())`,
  `ry = H * (0.1 + 0.1 * rng.next())`, `rx = ry * (2 + 2 * rng.next())`; a pixel is painted when its middle is
  inside the ellipse `(x, y, rx, ry)` or the smaller one `(x - 0.4 * rx, y - 0.6 * ry, 0.5 * rx, 0.8 * ry)`, each
  also tried moved by `-W` and `W`.

Every kind repeats sideways with no seam: `hills` because `a` and `b` are whole, the others because each shape is
also painted one width to each side. These numbers are a first look: Task 6 puts the painted pictures on
`ci-screens` and tunes them there **before** it records the golden.

### Task 7: The clip a character plays

**Files:**
- Create: `src/features/level/sprite.ts`, `clip.ts`
- Modify: `src/features/level/hero.ts`, `spawn-level.ts`, `game-sim.ts`, `level.ts`
- Test: `tests/features/level/clip.test.ts`, `sprite.test.ts`, `sprite.parity.test.ts`; `tests/golden/sprites.json`

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `Character.clips`, `Clip` (Task 2).
- Produces: `Sprite`, `spritesPlugin`, `usesSprites`, `setClip`, `setFacing` (the contract); from `clip.ts`:
  - `motionClip(vx: number, vy: number, now: string, still: number, landing: boolean): 'idle' | 'run' | 'jump' | 'fall' | 'land'`
  - `pickClip(clips: readonly Clip[], wanted: string): Clip | undefined`
  - `poseAt(clip: Clip, ticks: number): { pose: string; done: boolean }`
  - `characterParts(character, at, sprites: boolean)` and `heroParts` give the `Sprite` part when `sprites` is true:
    `{ character: id, player: 0 or the hero's number, face: 1 }`.

**The rules.** `motionClip` (y points down, speeds in tiles a second): `vy < -0.25` is `jump`; `vy > 0.25` is
`fall`; otherwise the character is hardly moving up or down, and: while `still` is under 4 and `now` is `jump` or
`fall`, it is `now` (the top of a jump is not the ground); else `land` when `now` is `fall`, or is `land` and
`landing`; else `run` when `|vx| > 0.01`; else `idle`. `still` is how many ticks in a row `|vy|` has been 0.25 or
less; the system counts it and stops at 4, so it stops changing. The numbers suit the default gravity.
`pickClip`: the clip called `wanted`; if there is none, the first that exists of: `land` gives way to `idle`;
`fall` to `jump`; `push` to `run`; then `idle`; then the character's first clip; `undefined` only for no clips.
`poseAt`: `i = floor(ticks * fps / 60)`; a looping clip shows `poses[i % n]` and is never done; another shows
`poses[min(i, n - 1)]` and is done when `i >= n`.

**The system** `level.sprites`, phase `cleanup`, for each entity with `Sprite` and `Body`, lowest id first: `face`
becomes 1 when `vx > 0.01` and −1 when `vx < -0.01`; the wanted clip is `by` when it is not empty, else
`motionClip(vx, vy, clip, still, the clip playing is not done)`; `pickClip` gives the clip; when its id is not `clip`,
`clip` and `since` (this tick) change; `pose` is `poseAt(clip, tick - since).pose`. A character with no clips keeps
`clip` and `pose` empty. A field is written only when it changes, so a still character sends the renderer nothing.

`createGameSim` adds `spritesPlugin` after `levelPlugin` when `usesSprites(content)`, and `spawnLevel` and
`joinPlugin` pass the same answer to `characterParts` and `heroParts`.

### Task 10: The backdrop and the camera

**Files:**
- Create: `src/features/render/camera.ts`
- Modify: `src/features/render/stage.ts`, `render.ts`, `scene.ts`
- Test: `tests/features/render/camera.test.ts`, `art.browser.test.ts` (added to)

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `Fit` from `fit.ts`; `View.sprite.player` (Task 9); `Level.view`, `BackdropFile` (Task 3).
- Produces: `Shot`; `Renderer.look`, `Renderer.heroes` (the contract); and, pure:
  - `frameHeroes(level: { w: number; h: number }, view: { w: number; h: number }, heroes: readonly { x: number; y: number }[]): Shot`
  - `easeShot(from: Shot, to: Shot): Shot`
  - `placeCamera(box: { w: number; h: number }, level: { w: number; h: number }, shot: Shot): Fit`
  - `layerShift(drift: number, fit: Fit, level: { w: number; h: number }, box: { w: number; h: number }): { x: number; y: number }`

**The camera.** `frameHeroes`: with no heroes, the level's middle at the view's size. Otherwise take the smallest
box round the heroes, 2 tiles wider on every side; the shot is as wide as the wider of the view and that box, and
as tall as the taller, but never wider or taller than the level; its middle is the box's middle, moved as little as
it must be to keep the shot inside the level. A hero whose place is not a number is left out. `easeShot` moves each of the four numbers 0.15 of the way. `placeCamera`: the
scale is the largest at which the whole shot fits the box; whatever more of the level then fits is shown too; along
a side where the level is smaller than what the screen shows it is centred, and along the other the screen never
shows past the level's edge. A box or a level with no size gives `{ scale: 0, left: 0, top: 0 }`, as `fitWorld`
does.

The renderer takes one camera step per `apply`: the first goes straight to `frameHeroes`, later ones ease to it;
`draw(alpha)` blends the last two, as it blends things. `look({ x, y, zoom })` puts the camera exactly there from
the next `apply`, at the view's size divided by `zoom` (the whole level's size when the level has no `view`);
`look(undefined)` eases back to the heroes. A level with no `view` and no `look` is `fitWorld`, to the pixel, as
today. `heroes()` is every view with `sprite.player` above 0 as its box on the canvas in CSS pixels, lowest player
first.

**The backdrop.** Each layer is its strip, repeated sideways to cover the whole canvas (the bars beside a level
included), `width` tiles wide and `height` tiles tall at the level's scale. `layerShift` says where a layer's
bottom-left corner is: with `drift` 1 it is the level's bottom-left corner; with `drift` 0 it stays where that
corner would be if the camera were at the level's left and bottom; between the two it is the blend. A prop is its
figure's pose, standing at `x` and `up` on its layer and moving with it. With no camera every layer stands on the
level's bottom edge.
